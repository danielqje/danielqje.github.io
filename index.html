About Me

As a little kid I always had an interest in the heavens, something about the vastness of space and all the celestial objects staring at me from the night sky intrigued me. In elemntray school trips to the school library I always chose the space books, especially a book on Jupiter (my favorite planet), my biggest fascination as a child, the Moon, my favorite celestial object, always seemed to follow me everywhere I went, and it was most noticeable while I was in the backseat of a driving car and I always wondered, "how is it follow me everywhere I go and why does its velocity change to match mine", well now I know it's because its enormous distance creates negligible parallax so my changing position produces almost no apparent angular shift in the sky.

I am an undergraduate student at San Diego State University (SDSU) pursuing a Bachelor of Science (B.S.) in Astronomy with a Minor in Mathematics. My interests span galaxies, stellar populations, compact objects, observational astronomy, and data analysis, with a growing interest in using spectroscopy and scientific computing to study astrophysical systems.

My current research experience has focused on stellar absorption features in elliptical galaxy spectra, particularly the Na I D doublet and how stellar populations can affect interpretations of gas inflows. I enjoy combining astronomy, physics, mathematics, and Python to better understand the physical processes behind what we observe in the universe. Outside my research I am especially fascinated by neutron stars, black holes, and the evolution of galaxies. My long-term goal is to continue into graduate research in astronomy or astrophysics and contribute to observational and computational research.
