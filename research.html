Research

Stellar Na I D Absorption in Elliptical Galaxies

STARTAstro Summer Research Program — University of California, San Diego (UCSD) & San Diego State University (SDSU)

Conducted research under the mentorship of Dr. Kate Rubin through UCSD and SDSU, with the majority of the work carried out at SDSU.

My current research investigates how stellar populations contribute to the Na I D absorption feature in elliptical galaxy spectra and how this stellar absorption can affect the interpretation of gas inflows.

Research Motivation

Elliptical galaxies show evidence of inflowing gas, and the Na I D absorption feature can be used to trace that gas. However, stars also naturally produce Na I D absorption, which complicates the interpretation. Because the stellar Na I D feature changes with the alpha-to-iron abundance ratio, the stellar contribution must be modeled accurately before excess Na I D absorption can be interpreted as evidence of inflowing gas.

Methods

  • Analyzed simple stellar population model spectra
  • Examined changes in Na I D absorption across different alpha-to-iron abundance ratios
  • Converted wavelength measurements from air wavelengths to vacuum wavelengths
  • Isolated the Na I D spectral region
  • Performed continuum fitting around the Na I D region using Legendre polynomials
  • Performed continuum normalization of the spectra
  • Modeled the Na I D doublet using two Gaussian components
  • Developed manual double-Gaussian trial models to examine the behavior of the Na I D absorption feature
  • Optimized the Gaussian parameters using SciPy curve_fit
  • Calculated ratios between the continuum-normalized spectra and optimized Gaussian models
  • Used ratio residuals to identify absorption not fully reproduced by the Gaussian stellar model
  • Compared the behavior of the models across [α/Fe] = -0.2, 0.0, 0.2, 0.4, and 0.6

Tools

Python • NumPy • Matplotlib • SciPy (curve_fit) • Astropy • Specutils • Jupyter

5. Ratio & Excess Absorption Analysis

After obtaining the optimized Gaussian models, I divided each continuum-normalized spectrum by its corresponding optimized fit. A ratio of one indicates agreement between the spectrum and model, while regions below one indicate absorption stronger than the fitted stellar model.
